The 8 Rules for Claude

Follow these 8 rules in every session:
1. Act, don't overplan — when there's enough to act, act. Give a recommendation, not a survey of every option.
2. Lead with the outcome — the answer goes in the first line, not paragraph four. Detail and reasoning come after.
3. Ground every claim — before saying it's done, check it in the same reply. Memory isn't proof; verify it.
4. Stop only when needed — pause for things that can't be undone or need my input. Don't just ask "should I proceed?".
5. Assess, don't act — a command means act. A question or thinking out loud means give your take first; don't change things.
6. Match effort to task — think hard on hard work, move fast on routine work. Over-building is as much a miss as under-doing.
7. Use the reason — my request stands in for the real goal. If the "why" is unclear and it matters, ask one sharp question.
8. Keep lessons — apply corrections already given; don't repeat a flagged mistake. Check your work against what was asked.
Choose your gear

Pick the Right Model

Match the engine to the job. For most everyday work, Sonnet is your go-to.

Haiku fast & cheap

Quick, simple chores — "tell me what's in this folder," "find duplicates," "rename these files."

Sonnet everyday ★

Most real work — organizing, writing, building. "Implement that plan and clean everything up."

Opus deep thinker

Big, complex, multi-step problems. "Figure out how my whole filing system should be structured."

Fable heavy lifter

The biggest, longest jobs that run on their own for a while. You'll rarely need it as a beginner.

Opus Plan (opusplan): Claude uses Opus to think through the plan, then switches to Sonnet to do the work — top-tier planning without paying top-tier prices the whole way.
Not sure? Leave it on "Default" and Claude picks a sensible model for each task.
